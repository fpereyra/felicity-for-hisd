## 1. Item 11 lowers the special-education math target and converts the reading target to a floor below current performance, on consent, with the cover sheet not listing those measures — **Oversight-reducing; procedurally irregular**
- **What:** GPM 4.2 (SWD math growth) 2028 target 58% → 55%. GPM 4.1 (SWD reading growth) "48% → 55% by 2028" → "remain at 55% or above." Current attainment: 4.2 = 51 (Not Met vs 52); 4.1 = 56 (down from 58). Cover sheet lists nine measures "impacted"; 4.1 and 4.2 are not among them but are changed in the attachment (pp102–104). Nov 2025 proposal said 4.1/4.2 "do not require revision."
- **Where:** Item 11, consent, pp102–105; monitoring data item 3, pp54–61.
- **Why:** Special-education compliance is one of TEA's three exit criteria (HPM 08-17, Chronicle 08-25). Lowering the SPED math bar and freezing the reading bar the same month the district reports Goal 4 "Not Met" and 4.2 "Not Met" moves the goalposts toward the district. It happens on consent with the cover sheet silent on the two SPED growth measures.
- **Counter:** NWEA changed norms, linking study, and the EISA algorithm; re-baselining is legitimate (and the November packet said so). The 4.1 floor of 55 equals the existing 2028 endpoint. 4.3 is being reinstated.
- **Her answer:** Re-baselining explains changing the *metric*; it does not explain lowering the *2028 endpoint* for a growth measure the district itself said last November needed no revision. If the district believes the 2025-26 value is inflated by the norm change, the slide should say so instead of headlining a 31% gain from it. Ask: pull item 11 from consent, or at minimum amend the cover sheet to disclose 4.1 and 4.2.

## 6. EHBB(LOCAL) bans socioeconomic status as a scoring value in GT identification, attributed to TEA without citation — **Inequitable; contrary to research (conditional)**
- **What:** New: "the use of a scoring value based on race, ethnicity, sex, socioeconomic status, or disability shall be prohibited." Deleted: the placement committee's duty to "evaluate each referred student." Cover: "to align with updated policies from TEA."
- **Where:** Item 12, pp106–111.
- **Why:** Race/sex prohibitions are readily grounded in law. SES is not a protected class, and SES-sensitive identification (local norms, universal screening) is the main evidence-based lever for closing GT identification gaps: universal screening raised identification of low-income and Black/Hispanic students substantially in Broward County (Card & Giuliano, 2016, PNAS), and local-norm approaches are recommended by Peters, Rambo-Hernandez, Makel, Matthews & Plucker (2019). The literature is contested on *how* to weight, not on whether SES-blind cutoffs under-identify low-income students.
- **Counter:** The prohibition tracks TEA's revised State Plan/rule language post-89th Legislature; universal screening is still permitted; HISD's matrix may not use SES today, so nothing changes in practice.
- **Her answer:** Ask for the citation (which TEA rule or statute prohibits SES as a scoring value), the current matrix, and GT identification rates by economic status for 2025-26. If the matrix does not use SES, the district can say so and the flag closes. If it does, the board is voting to remove the one input that offsets the gap.
